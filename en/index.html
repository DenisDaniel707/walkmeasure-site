How it works

Set the phone down. Feel the buzz. Move on.

Like a dedicated measuring device, WalkMeasure follows the phone's movement with its accelerometer and gyroscope. Every time the phone rests still on the ground, the app corrects the error picked up on the way — which is why it measures well in short hops with frequent stops.

  1. Clip the phone onto a selfie stick

    You don't have to, but it's far more comfortable: you measure standing up, at your own pace. In the app, turn on the "Selfie stick" option.

  2. Set it down and let it rest

    At the first corner, the phone lies flat on the ground, still, for about a second. Keep the stick steady — the app records the point by itself.

  3. Feel the buzz through the handle — move on

    The phone gives a long, strong buzz, made to be felt through the selfie stick. The app knows it's its own vibration and doesn't mistake it for movement.

  4. Every metre or more

    Carry the phone to the next point and set it down again — at every corner, and as often as you like along the way. Frequent stops keep the error small; if you carry it too long without a stop, the app warns you with a beep.

  5. Finish where you started

    Bring the phone back to the starting point. The outline closes, and the measured gap between start and finish shows you, in real terms, how accurate the measurement was.

First time: calibrate. It takes a few minutes, you do it once, and it improves accuracy a lot. The app guides you step by step.

What WalkMeasure is — and isn't

It is

  • A quick estimate, with its margin of error shown
  • A tool for quotes, planning, checks and personal projects
  • A first check before you call in a professional

It isn't

  • A cadastral survey or a legally valid document
  • A replacement for a licensed surveyor for land registration, subdivision, boundary disputes or building permits
  • A millimetre-precision instrument

About accuracy, plainly

A phone's motion sensors aren't made for surveying. WalkMeasure makes up for it with frequent stops, calibration and the start–finish check, but the error grows the longer you carry the phone between stops. That's why the app always shows the margin of error, not just the number.

The accuracy figures so far come from computer simulations with an assumed sensor model — not from real-world measurements. In the field, the result depends on the phone, the calibration and how still the phone rests at each point. We'll publish field results here, compared against a tape measure and a professional survey.

FAQ

Good to know

Do I need internet or GPS?

No. The app uses only the iPhone's accelerometer and gyroscope. It works offline, indoors, under trees and anywhere GPS is weak.

Does it use the camera?

No. It never uses the camera and never asks for access to it. The only permission it asks for is motion sensors.

How accurate is it?

It depends on how often you set the phone down and on calibration. The app shows the margin of error with every result and checks it for real when you return to the starting point. The figures published so far come from simulations; field results will appear on this page. For critical dimensions, check with a tape measure.

Can I use the result for the land registry or a sale?

No. Official documents need a measurement by a licensed professional. WalkMeasure quickly tells you what to expect, so you go to the surveyor prepared.

Why a selfie stick?

So you don't have to bend down at every point. Clip the phone into the clamp, set it flat on the ground, keep the stick steady for about a second, and the buzz in the handle tells you to move on. Turn on "Selfie stick" in the app so it knows the phone is on the ground even though your hand still holds the stick. A rigid stick with no wobble gives better results.

Do I have to stop every metre?

Set the phone down at every corner and, on long sides, every metre or every few metres. Each stop corrects the error picked up on the way. If you carry the phone too long without a stop, the app warns you.

Does it work indoors?

Yes. For rooms, hold the phone upright against each wall and the app builds the plan with walls, doors and windows.

Which phone do I need?

An iPhone running iOS 17 or later.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. Projects stay on the phone; there's no account, no analytics and no ads. You share them yourself, as PDF, CSV or DXF, only when you want to.
